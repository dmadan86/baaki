# Drafts and rules: transactions that arrive by themselves, and a rule that files them

A plan, written against a screen recording of Splitkaro: a **Drafts** screen with
three lanes — _SMS Drafts_, _SMS Rules_, _Offline Drafts_ — plus an "Auto fetch"
toggle, an "Added from SMS" tag on the ledger, and a banner offering to bring in
133 older transactions.

The short version, before the detail:

- **The SMS half can ship on Android, and can never ship on iPhone.** Google
  Play has an approved use case that describes this app — _SMS-based money
  management_ — so the inbox read is obtainable, at the price of a declaration,
  a store-listing edit and a standing promise that no message body leaves the
  device. iOS has no such API at any tier. So Android reads the inbox; iPhone
  pastes and shares. §1.
- **Almost every piece of the drafts half already exists** and is either dark or
  one screen away. The parser is written and tested. So is an _email statement_
  parser nobody has ever seen. `captures` is already the "a transaction with no
  group yet" row, and `capture.assign` is already the "Add to" button. §2.
- **The rules engine is the genuinely new thing**, it is independent of how
  transactions arrive, and it runs entirely on the device. §4.
- **Three lanes under one word is the wrong shape for Waves.** Two of the three
  already have homes; the third is a real gap that #786 opened last week. §5.

## 1. The permission verdict, first, because it has changed

### 1.1 What Waves declares today

Nothing. `apps/mobile/app.json:30` is literally:

```json
"permissions": [],
```

There is no `AndroidManifest.xml` in the repo for the app (it is generated by
`expo prebuild`), no SMS config plugin in `apps/mobile/app.config.ts`, and no
`PermissionsAndroid` call anywhere in `apps/mobile/src`. The app reads no message
log on any device.

`react-native-get-sms-android@2.1.0` **is still a declared dependency**
(`apps/mobile/package.json:70`, patched at
`patches/react-native-get-sms-android@2.1.0.patch` — the patch only swaps
`jcenter()` for `mavenCentral()`). It is imported by nothing. Its own library
manifest declares no permissions (only its bundled _example_ app does), so it is
not currently leaking `READ_SMS` into a merged manifest. An earlier revision of
this document called it dead weight and scheduled its removal; it is now the
starting point for §9's Android lane, and it stays.

### 1.2 What the policy actually says

**There is an approved use case, and it is this app.**

Google Play's _Use of SMS or Call Log permission groups_ page lists the permitted
uses in a table. One row, quoted verbatim:

> **Use:** SMS-based money management
> **Description:** "For example, apps that track and manage budget"
> **Eligible permissions:** `READ_SMS, RECEIVE_MMS, RECEIVE_SMS, RECEIVE_WAP_PUSH`

Both permissions this feature would want — the inbox read and the live receive —
are eligible, under a case that describes Waves in six words.

> **Correction, recorded rather than quietly fixed.** An earlier revision of this
> section said personal finance and expense tracking were "not on the list" and
> concluded the permission was unobtainable at any price. **That was wrong.** It
> was drawn from the compliance playbook's `rejection-patterns.json`, which
> records the _rejection_ pattern — a restricted permission declared with no
> approved use case — and does not enumerate the approved cases. The live policy
> page does. Everything that followed from that error in the earlier revision —
> the "fatal" verdict, the removal-first Phase 0 — has been rewritten here. The
> rest of the document (the rules engine, the drafts hub, the data model) never
> depended on it and stands unchanged.

The precedent is not theoretical: Splitkaro (`com.bsquare.splitkaro`) ships this
exact feature, on Play, at roughly 340k downloads. The recording that started this
plan is of an app doing a permitted thing.

Also changing, though not for us: from **27 January 2027**, account verification
by phone call is no longer a permitted use of `READ_CALL_LOG`. Waves requests no
call-log permission and is unaffected. It is noted because it shows the list is
actively maintained — a case that exists today can be narrowed tomorrow, which is
the entire argument for §9's kill switch.

### 1.3 What the declaration actually costs

Approval is not a form you file and forget. The policy attaches conditions, and
each one is a build requirement rather than a paragraph:

| the policy says                                                                                                                                | what it obliges in this repo                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Only access Call Log or SMS permissions when your app falls within permitted uses and only to enable your app's critical core functionality." | The reader may only ever produce drafts for the expense inbox — no other caller, now or later. Minimum necessary: read on demand for a bounded window, and hold no live receiver unless §9's auto-fetch phase is separately declared. |
| "Make sure that your app's description prominently documents and promotes its core feature(s)."                                                | The **Play listing text must describe SMS expense import before the declaration is filed.** This is a store-listing edit the account owner makes, and it is a prerequisite, not a follow-up.                                          |
| User Data Policy — Prominent Disclosure and Consent                                                                                            | A disclosure screen **before** the system permission dialog, in the app's own words, saying what is read and what becomes of it. Not a rationale string bolted onto the system prompt. Four locales, like everything else.            |
| Spyware Policy — no exfiltration of non-compliant data                                                                                         | **A message body must never leave the device.** See §1.4. This is the condition that would end the app, and it is also the one Waves is already built to satisfy.                                                                     |
| Privacy Policy                                                                                                                                 | Must name SMS as a data source and state that it is processed on-device.                                                                                                                                                              |

### 1.4 The one that would actually end the app

Parsing happens on-device today. `packages/core/src/sms/parse.ts` is a pure
function: text in, a structured candidate out, no network. That is not a
convenient accident — it is the single most valuable compliance property this
feature has, and it now has to be defended on purpose rather than left true by
luck:

- A raw message body must never reach Sentry, Clarity, or any analytics event.
  Parser failures may report **shape** — did it match, which bank format, a
  confidence bucket — and never text. §10 already said this for parser-quality
  reasons; it is restated because the reason is now a policy one.
- `captures.raw_text` is the one column that would hold a message body, and a
  capture **syncs to the server**. Two things must be settled before the reader
  ships, and neither is settled today:
  1. whether `raw_text` is covered by the at-rest mirror encryption (#475) on the
     way up, and
  2. whether an SMS-derived capture should send `raw_text` **at all**, given that
     the parsed fields are what the inbox actually renders.

  My recommendation is the blunt one: **a silently-read SMS capture syncs its
  parsed fields and leaves `raw_text` on the device.** A bank message body sitting
  in a server-side column is precisely the shape the Spyware Policy exists to
  describe, and nothing in the drafts hub needs it. A _pasted_ message is the
  person's own deliberate act and can keep today's behaviour; one the app took
  from the inbox by itself should not.

### 1.7 iOS has no equivalent, at any tier

There is no API on iOS that reads the Messages database. Not a restricted one, not
an entitlement, not a paid tier. Every inbox-reading screen is Android-only, and
any design that makes the inbox read the _spine_ of this feature ships a
materially different app on iPhone.

Apple's own guideline points where the alternatives already do — `APPLE.md:90`,
5.1.1(iii): _"Collect the minimum, **use the system picker or share sheet instead
of full access**."_

### 1.8 The verdict, stated plainly

> **Android may read the inbox. iPhone never will.** The permission is obtainable
> under _SMS-based money management_, and the app it would make is the one in the
> recording. It costs a Play declaration, a store-listing edit that has to land
> first, a disclosure screen, and a standing obligation that no message body ever
> leaves the phone.
>
> So the feature gets built in that order: **the paste, share and statement lanes
> first**, because they are the entire feature on iPhone and the fallback on
> Android; **then the inbox read on top**, Android-only, behind the
> `sms_inbox_read` flag that already exists — so that if the case is ever
> narrowed, one server flag retires the lane and nobody loses the app.
>
> Everything downstream of a parsed transaction — the drafts hub, "Add to",
> provenance, the entire rules engine — is platform-neutral, permission-free, and
> mostly already built.

### 2.1 The three findings that change the shape of this plan

**`captures` is already the SMS-drafts row.** Column for column:

```sql
CREATE TABLE public.captures (
    id uuid NOT NULL,              owner_user_id uuid NOT NULL,
    description text DEFAULT '',   category text,
    expense_date date NOT NULL,    currency text NOT NULL,
    amount bigint NOT NULL,        notes text,
    photo_path text,               raw_text text,
    parsed jsonb,                  status text DEFAULT 'open',
    assigned_expense_id uuid,      assigned_group_id uuid,
    payment_method text,           target_group_id uuid,
    category_meta jsonb,           location jsonb,
    updated_seq bigint, created_at, updated_at, deleted_at,
    CONSTRAINT captures_amount_nonneg CHECK (amount >= 0),
    CONSTRAINT captures_status_check CHECK (status IN ('open','assigned'))
);
```

`raw_text` holds the message. `parsed` holds what came out of it. `description`
holds the merchant. `payment_method` holds `'credit'` when the SMS said card.
`target_group_id` is "where I mean this to go, decided later". `status` is
Not Added / Added. There is no merchant column and none is needed —
`description` is it, and `normaliseMerchantName` is the tokeniser.

**The email parser exists and nobody has ever seen it.** `import/email.ts`
handles the case the SMS parser cannot: a statement is a _table_, many rows, one
document-level date. Its options make `from`/`to` optional, so unlike
`proposeFromSms` it does not require a trip window. It is Play-safe, works on
iPhone, and delivers exactly the "133 older transactions" backfill the recording
advertises — a month of statement, pasted once.

**Dedupe is already solved twice over.** `dedupeKey(parsed, at)`
(`sms/parse.ts:326`) prefers the bank's own reference, falling back to
amount+day+merchant, and `import/email.ts` reuses it, _"so a transaction that
arrives once by SMS and once on the month's statement is one candidate, not
two."_ And the deleted `importId.ts` shows the durable half: derive the
`clientMutationId` from the thing itself, and `expenses.client_mutation_id`
UNIQUE refuses a re-import at the only layer that decides. The same trick works
for a capture id, because capture ids are client-chosen.

## 3. Where each piece is evaluated, and why

`docs/notifications-and-email-strategy.md:46` states the rule this repo already
lives by:

> Anything derived from the private personal ledger is scheduled on the device.
> It never becomes a `notifications` row.

and, one table further down, it classifies captures the same way:

> | captures (an expense with no group yet) | `captures`, plaintext, but scoped
> to one `owner_user_id` | **local** — it is one person's unfiled spending, and
> the server has no second party with an interest |

That settles the architecture before it is designed.

| piece                                              | runs where                                                               | why                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Parsing a message or a statement                   | **device**, pure                                                         | already true and already documented — `sms/parse.ts` and `import/email.ts` touch no network, are called by no edge function (ADR-013). A statement carries a card number and a running balance; parsing it locally is the point |
| Evaluating a rule against a draft                  | **device**                                                               | the input is a capture, which the doc above classifies local; and the matching happens before any group exists, so there is no second party                                                                                     |
| Evaluating a rule against a personal-ledger record | **device, necessarily**                                                  | `supabase/functions/sync/index.ts:1226` validates `recordKind` and stores `data` verbatim. The server cannot read the blob and must not start                                                                                   |
| Learning from a manual move                        | **device**                                                               | it is one person's private preference about their own spending                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Applying a rule's result                           | device enqueues `capture.update` / `expense.create` like any other write | nothing new                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Notifying "3 drafts are waiting"                   | **device**, local notification                                           | `apps/mobile/src/lib/captureNudge/plan.ts` already does exactly this, and the strategy doc lists _"LOCAL — unassigned captures waiting"_ at §4.3                                                                                |
| Storing rules                                      | server relays the rows, never reads them                                 | §6                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

**No rule can be evaluated server-side, and none should be.** That is not a
limitation to work around; it is the same answer the two recent docs reached
independently, and it makes the engine a pure function in `packages/core` —
deterministic, same answer on every device (ADR-009), testable with no database.

## 4. The rules engine

### 4.1 Shape

A rule is `when <every condition matches> then <these actions>`, ordered, with
the first match winning unless a rule is marked "keep going".

```ts
// packages/core/src/rules/types.ts
export interface AutomationRule {
  readonly id: string;
  readonly enabled: boolean;
  readonly priority: number; // lower runs first; learned rules sort after authored ones
  readonly learned: boolean; // true when Waves proposed it, false when a person wrote it
  readonly when: readonly Condition[];
  readonly then: readonly Action[];
  readonly hits: number; // how many drafts it has filed
  readonly correctedAt: string | null;
  readonly createdAt: string;
}
```

**Conditions.** Every one of these is a field the parser already produces or a
column `captures` already has.

| condition       | matches on                                                      | source                                                            |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `merchant`      | one or more normalised tokens, compared as whole tokens         | `normaliseMerchantName(description)` — `category/merchant.ts:172` |
| `amountBetween` | `{ min, max }` in minor units, plus a currency                  | `captures.amount` / `captures.currency`, bigint, never a float    |
| `direction`     | debit / credit                                                  | `TransactionDirection`, `sms/parse.ts`                            |
| `accountTail`   | the last 3–6 digits of the card or account                      | `ParsedSms.accountTail`                                           |
| `paymentMethod` | `'cash' \| 'upi' \| 'credit' \| 'debit' \| 'forex'`             | `protocol.ts:136`, already on `captures.payment_method`           |
| `source`        | `'sms' \| 'email' \| 'shared' \| 'voice' \| 'scan' \| 'manual'` | new, §6                                                           |
| `category`      | one of `CategoryId`, or a custom tag id                         | `captures.category`                                               |

**Actions.**

| action                   | effect                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `routeToGroup(groupId)`  | sets `captures.target_group_id` — the existing "where I mean this to go" field. The split is still decided at assignment; nothing about ADR-004 changes                                                    |
| `routeToPersonal()`      | marks it for the Me tab rather than a group                                                                                                                                                                |
| `setCategory(id, meta?)` | writes `category` + `category_meta`, exactly as the picker does                                                                                                                                            |
| `hold()`                 | leave it in the inbox for a person (**the default**)                                                                                                                                                       |
| `file()`                 | create the expense without asking — off by default, §4.3                                                                                                                                                   |
| `ignore()`               | soft-delete the draft on arrival. The reason this earns its place: a credit-card **bill payment** SMS and a self-transfer are debits that are not expenses, and without `ignore` the inbox fills with them |

### 4.2 Learning from one move

This is the feature that makes it feel like magic, and it is the one that can
most easily feel like possession. The design principle: **Waves may propose a
habit; it may never acquire one quietly.**

**What is remembered is the normalised merchant token string**, not the raw
message and not the display description:

```
"UPI-CRED CLUB-CRED@axisb-…"  →  merchantTokens()  →  ["cred", "club"]
"POS UPI SWIGGY*ORDER 8842"   →  normaliseMerchantName()  →  "swiggy order"
```

`normaliseMerchantName` already strips `POS`, `UPI`, `IMPS`, `NEFT`, gateway
brands, corporate suffixes and pure-digit runs
(`category/merchant.ts:36-69`), and its tests pin exactly these two examples
(`packages/core/test/merchant.test.ts:19-20`). Remembering the raw string would
learn a rule that matches one transaction and never fires again, because the
reference number is in it.

**When a rule is born.** After a person moves a draft into a group by hand, the
confirmation row gains one line:

> Put **CRED Club** in **Flat** from now on? — _Yes_ · _No thanks_

One tap, dismissable, shown once per merchant. Not a silent write. The
alternative — learning on the first move with no prompt, as the recording
implies — is the version that produces "why is my rent in the Goa trip", and it
is the version that cannot be reasoned about afterwards.

> **Open question O1.** Ask on the first move, or learn silently on the _second_
> consistent move and tell them afterwards? The second is closer to the
> competitor and to "it just works"; the first is closer to how the rest of
> Waves behaves (`categoryChosen` in `add-expense.tsx:799` already refuses to
> re-guess over a person's choice).

**How a wrong lesson is unlearned.** Three mechanisms, in order of how likely
they are to be used:

1. Every draft a rule touched says so, on the row: _"Flat — by a rule"_, with the
   rule's name tappable. Nothing is ever silently placed.
2. Moving a rule-placed draft somewhere else **rewrites the rule's target** and
   stamps `correctedAt`. Last move wins, which is what a person means by moving
   it.
3. A rule corrected three times without ever being accepted is **disabled, not
   rewritten**, and the Rules screen says why: _"This kept guessing wrong, so it
   has stopped."_ A rule that flip-flops is worse than no rule.

Deleting a learned rule is one tap and needs no confirmation — it created no
data of its own.

### 4.3 What a rule may never do by default

`then: [file()]` — create an expense with no human in the loop — is **off by
default and gated behind an explicit per-rule switch**, for one reason worth
writing down: a group expense is a claim against other people's money. A rule
that silently posts ₹4,200 to a shared flat ledger has spent somebody else's
evening. `hold()` is the default action of the default rule, and the inbox is
where held things live.

### 4.4 The default rule Waves should _not_ have

The recording's default is _"WHEN any transaction THEN Move to group · My
Personal [Primary]"_ — everything lands in a personal holding group first, for
review.

Waves has no such group, and it does not need one: **the captures inbox is
already that holding pen**, and it is better, because a capture carries no
members, no split and no balance until somebody assigns it
(`schema.prisma:936` — _"Personal until assigned"_). Splitkaro needs a Personal
group because its drafts have to be _somewhere_. Waves' drafts are nowhere by
construction, which is the stronger version of the same idea.

So: **no default rule.** The Rules screen opens with an explanatory empty state,
not a pre-made row.

## 5. The Drafts hub

### 5.1 Recommendation: do not build the three-lane hub

Three reasons, in order of weight.

**One: two of the three lanes are not the same kind of thing.** An SMS draft is
something you have not decided about. An offline draft is something you _have_
decided and the phone has not delivered. Filing them under one word teaches that
unsent work is undecided work, which is the opposite of what Waves promises — the
whole point of `pending_mutations` being on disk and overlaid on every read
(`packages/core/src/sync/mirror.ts:1-16`) is that a queued expense is a _real_
expense that happens to be in transit.

**Two: Waves already removed a second inbox for this exact reason.** The
notification Inbox screen was deleted in #565 because the app had two things
called an inbox and only one of them was where people looked. Adding "SMS
Drafts" as a list parallel to `/captures` — same row shape, same "Add to"
button, same badge — recreates that mistake with new names.

**Three: a left rail is not a Waves idiom and is an RTL hazard.** Every
multi-view screen in this app uses `SegmentedTabs` across the top
(`group/[id]/index.tsx:1138`, `personal/budgets.tsx:225`,
`expense/[expenseId].tsx:466`). A vertical rail pinned to the left edge has to be
mirrored for Arabic, and direction is decided natively at launch.

### 5.2 What to build instead — three lanes, three right homes

**Lane 1 — SMS/email drafts fold into the captures inbox.**
`apps/mobile/src/app/captures.tsx` already is the drafts screen. It gains:

- a `SegmentedTabs` source filter across the top: **All · Scanned · Spoken ·
  From messages** (shown only when more than one source is present, so it does
  not appear for a person who has never pasted anything);
- an **Added** tab beside **Waiting**. The inbox today filters
  `openCaptures()` (`mirror.ts:902`) and throws assigned rows away; showing them
  is a filter change, not new data. This is the recording's _Not Added (134) /
  Added_ pair;
- a provenance line on each row — _"From a message · HDFC"_ — reading
  `parsed.source`;
- **free multi-select** for assignment. `planCaptureAssign`
  (`captureBulkAssign.ts:110`) already builds one `expense.create` per capture
  and tolerates partial failure; today it is only reachable for one voice
  utterance's cluster. Generalising the selection is the only change, and it is
  what makes a pasted statement bearable.

**Lane 2 — Rules get their own screen, hung off the inbox, not settings.** A
rule's whole job is to decide where a draft goes, so it belongs beside the
drafts: `apps/mobile/src/app/captures/rules.tsx`, reached from the inbox
header's `···`. Not under `personal/` — that section is behind `PersonalGuard`
(`apps/mobile/src/components/PersonalGuard.tsx`), and needing a fingerprint to
edit where your Swiggy orders go would be absurd.

**Lane 3 — "Offline Drafts" becomes one small list, and #786 is what earns it.**
This is the lane I expected to argue against and cannot.

Today, what a person can see about unsent work is `SyncBanner.tsx`: a header
glyph that renders **nothing at all** when online and idle (line 121,
`if (!state) return null;`), and, when something is wrong, a sheet that names
only the _head_ refused or stuck item. There is no enumeration anywhere, and
`settings/sync.tsx` is only a Wi-Fi/mobile-data preference.

Last week that became a real gap. #786 (`apps/mobile/src/sync/retention.ts`)
established that when a session ends _involuntarily_ — a revoked token, a
refresh expired past recovery, a sign-out from another device — the phone
**keeps** the mutation queue and its encryption key for up to 30 days
(`RETENTION_WINDOW_MS`), because that work exists nowhere else in the world.
Which means Waves can now hold a person's unsent expenses for a month while
showing them, in the UI, exactly nothing.

So: **a list, under `settings/sync`, titled with the words that are already
translated** — `misc.notSentYet` ("Not sent yet"). One row per queued mutation:
what it is, when it was made, and its state (waiting · retrying in 2m · stuck ·
refused). `pendingMutations(queue)` and `deadLettered(queue)`
(`packages/core/src/sync/queue.ts:535,540`) already return exactly these; the
per-item **Try again** / **Discard it** buttons already exist in `SyncBanner`
and already respect _a refusal is not a deletion_ (`queue.ts:16-25`,
`discard()` at `queue.ts:609` cascades to the dependent `capture.assign`).

It is a screen, not a lane, and it is worth it — but call it what it is. Not
"Offline Drafts": **"Waiting to send"**.

### 5.3 The backfill banner, and what it costs

_"Auto-tracking is on — Add 133 older transactions too"_ is a promise the
inbox-read permission makes, and on Android §9's phase 4 can now keep it. On
iPhone the equivalent is the email statement: paste a month, get a month. Both
land in the same inbox and both run through the same rules, so this section
applies whichever way the rows arrived — the only difference is how many arrive
at once, and the phone that did it.

Three costs to name:

1. **Queue shape.** `MAX_MUTATIONS = 200` per `/sync` request and
   `MAX_ROWS_PER_TABLE = 500` per pull page
   (`supabase/functions/sync/index.ts:192,194`).
   133 captures is one request; a year of statements is not. Chunk the creation
   and let the existing drain loop pace it — do not add a bulk endpoint.
2. **Idempotence.** Derive each capture's id from `dedupeKey` the way
   `importId.ts` derived a mutation id, so pasting last month's statement twice
   produces the same ids and the second paste writes nothing. `import/email.ts`
   already caps at 300 candidates per paste (`maxCandidates`).
3. **Review load.** 133 rows that all need a group is not a feature, it is a
   chore. This is the one place the rules engine pays for itself immediately:
   run the rules over the batch first, then show the person the ~15 that no rule
   matched. That is the real product, and it is why the rules engine should land
   _before_ any high-volume import path.

### 5.4 Provenance on the ledger row

Two levels, and only one of them needs a migration.

**On the capture: none needed.** `parsed` is an untyped jsonb the client already
uses for exactly this kind of thing — `voiceBatchId` lives there
(`apps/mobile/src/lib/captureBatch.ts:12`). Add `source`, `sender`,
`dedupeKey`, `confidence`, `accountTail` alongside it. No DDL, no Prisma model,
no edge change (`createCapture` at `supabase/functions/sync/index.ts:835` passes
`parsed` through). It is sealed at rest on the device like every other mirror
json column (`apps/mobile/src/sync/rowCipher.ts`).

**On the expense: use the column that is already there.**
`expense_versions.source` is an `ExpenseSource` enum of `'manual' | 'imported'`,
and `waves_apply_expense` takes `p_source text DEFAULT 'manual'` — but
`packages/core/src/sync/expenseWrite.ts:219` records that _"`p_source` is left to
its default ('manual') by every caller, so it is not built here."_ Only
`waves_import_ledger` ever sets `'imported'`.

Recommendation: **plumb `source` through `expense-write`, set it to `'imported'`
when the expense came from a draft that came from a message, and do not add
enum values in v1.** The fine-grained provenance stays on the capture, which is
never hard-deleted and points at the expense through `assigned_expense_id`, so
"Added from SMS" resolves. Adding `'sms'` and `'email'` to a Postgres enum is
cheap but is a migration plus a deploy, and it buys a distinction the capture
already holds.

> **Open question O2.** Should the _other members of a group_ see that an
> expense arrived from someone's bank SMS? `expense_versions.source` is
> group-readable. "Added from SMS" as a badge everyone sees is a small leak of
> how one member manages their money. My instinct is that the badge is
> **private to the author**, rendered from the capture, and `source='imported'`
> stays an internal audit value. Confirm.

## 6. Data model and migrations

### 6.1 Rules: a new owner-scoped table

Follow `group_pins` (#789, `38525380`, merged five days ago) exactly. It is the
template: a new owner-scoped synced table, its Prisma model, its `updated_seq`
stamper, its RLS, its two mutation kinds, its mirror materialiser and its edge
handler, all in one PR of ~880 lines.

```sql
-- packages/db/prisma/migrations/<ts>_automation_rules/migration.sql
CREATE TABLE public.automation_rules (
    id            uuid    NOT NULL,
    owner_user_id uuid    NOT NULL,
    enabled       boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true,
    priority      integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 100,
    learned       boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,
    "when"        jsonb   NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'::jsonb,
    "then"        jsonb   NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'::jsonb,
    hits          integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    corrected_at  timestamptz,
    updated_seq   bigint  NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    created_at    timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    updated_at    timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    deleted_at    timestamptz
);
-- + PK, FK to profiles ON DELETE CASCADE ON UPDATE CASCADE,
-- + index (owner_user_id, updated_seq),
-- + waves_next_automation_rule_seq / waves_stamp_automation_rule_seq + trigger,
-- + RLS: owner-only, `owner_user_id = public.waves_current_profile_id()`.
```

**A new table needs a matching Prisma model or `pnpm db:drift` fails**
(`packages/db/package.json:14` — `prisma migrate diff --from-config-datasource
--to-schema --exit-code`). Write `model AutomationRule` in
`packages/db/prisma/schema.prisma` in the same commit. Note the column names
`when` / `then` are reserved-ish in SQL and must be quoted; if that reads badly,
`conditions` / `actions` are fine and cheaper to live with. **Prefer
`conditions` / `actions`.**

Alongside it:

- `packages/core/src/sync/protocol.ts` — `SyncTable.AutomationRules`,
  `MutationKind.RuleUpsert` / `RuleDelete`, `rulesScope(profileId)` →
  `` `${profileId}:rules` ``, following `groupPinsScope` at `protocol.ts:537`;
- `packages/core/src/sync/mirror.ts` — `materialiseRules`, queue overlaid on the
  mirror like every other entity;
- `supabase/functions/sync/index.ts` — `requireRulesScope` + two handlers, using
  `this.caller` not `this.service` so RLS is what holds (ADR-013: a definer
  function is for doing what your own grants would not allow, and writing a row
  you already own is not that).

**Why not `personal_records` with a fifth `record_kind`?** It is genuinely
cheaper — three edits: the CHECK constraint (`migration.sql:7916`), the union at
`protocol.ts:356`, and the validation array at
`supabase/functions/sync/index.ts:1227` — with no new table and no Prisma model
(Prisma does not represent CHECK constraints, so `db:drift` never sees them).
But `personal_records` is A56's table and carries a documented meaning — _the
private money ledger_ — and a routing rule is not a money record. Overloading it
buys three days and costs a boundary. The table is the right call.

> **Open question O3.** A plaintext `automation_rules` table tells the server
> _"this person sends anything from CRED Club to their flat group"_ — a
> behavioural profile it does not hold today. The alternative is to keep the
> rows but make `conditions`/`actions` an opaque blob the client seals, as
> `personal_records` effectively is. Is that worth the cost (no server-side
> anything, ever, including an admin debugging a report)? My default is
> plaintext, on the grounds that `captures.raw_text` already puts far more on
> the server. Confirm.

## 10. Risks

- **The approved use case is narrowed, or the declaration is refused.** This is
  now the top risk in the document, and it is not hypothetical: §1.2 records a
  use case being removed from the same list in January 2027. The mitigation is
  the shape of §9 rather than a comment — the Android lane is the last thing
  built, never the spine, and hangs off `sms_inbox_read`. Retiring it is a server
  flag, not a release. **Nothing should be built on the assumption that the inbox
  read is permanent.**
- **A message body reaches a server, an analytics event, or a crash report.**
  §1.4 is the whole of it. This is the failure that ends the app rather than the
  feature, and it deserves a test rather than a comment: assert that an
  SMS-sourced capture's sync payload carries no `raw_text`, and that the parser's
  failure reporting carries shape and never text.
- **The store listing and the declaration drift apart.** The policy requires the
  listing to promote the feature the permission serves. If the Android lane is
  later turned off by flag while the listing still advertises it, the app is
  describing a feature it does not have — a policy problem in the opposite
  direction. Whoever flips the flag owns the listing edit too.
- **Parser quality on real Indian bank SMS.** `parseSms` is dated 2026-08 and
  its date handling is day-first only (documented at `sms/parse.ts:169-172`).
  Paste is more forgiving than auto-fetch — the person sees what came out — but
  a low hit rate makes the feature feel broken. Collect failures behind the
  existing opt-in analytics, never the message text.
- **A learned rule that files into a shared group.** §4.3 keeps `file()` off by
  default. If it is ever turned on by default, this becomes the top risk in the
  document.
- **`decodeRecurring`-style lossy round-trips.** Not applicable if rules get
  their own table with real columns — which is one more reason for §6.1's
  recommendation over the opaque-blob alternative, since
  `docs/plan-recurring-money-events.md:249` documents exactly this hazard for
  `personal_records`: _"a field a future build adds does not survive today's
  decode."_
- **iOS/Android divergence, which is now deliberate and needs saying out loud.**
  Phases 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8 are identical on both. Phase 4 (and 7) exist only on
  Android, by platform limit rather than by choice — there is no iOS API to
  match them. Two consequences worth planning for rather than discovering:
  **the marketing cannot be shared** (an App Store listing must not advertise
  automatic SMS import), and **the app's own copy must not promise it either** —
  a settings row reading "Auto-import from SMS" that is simply absent on iPhone
  reads as a broken build, so the iOS surface should offer paste and share in its
  own words rather than hide an Android row. Phase 9 is the one that closes the
  gap as far as it can close: a Share Extension on iOS, an intent filter on
  Android, same feature, two implementations, and the iOS half needs an
  app-target change (`@bacons/apple-targets` is already in the plugin list).
- **Two writers on `capture.tsx`.** `feat/capture-asks-who-paid` is live in that
  file right now. Phases 1 and 2 touch `captures.tsx` and a new screen, not
  `capture.tsx`, but the i18n block is shared and will conflict.

## 11. Open questions for the user

**O0 — Auto fetch: is `RECEIVE_SMS` worth a second declaration?** The inbox
read (phase 4) and the live receive (phase 7) are separately declarable and
separately refusable, and the second puts a broadcast receiver on every message
that arrives. Phase 4 alone gets the drafts and the backfill, one tap later than
the recording. **Decided: Android reads the inbox, iPhone gets paste and share.**
Still open is whether the live receive follows, or whether an on-demand read is
enough. §1.6, §9 phase 7.

**O1 — When does a rule get learned?** Ask on the first manual move ("Put CRED
Club in Flat from now on?"), or learn silently on the second consistent move and
tell them after? The first is how the rest of Waves behaves; the second is what
the recording promises. §4.2.

**O2 — Is "Added from SMS" private to the author?** `expense_versions.source` is
readable by every member of the group. Should the other members see that your
expense came from your bank's text message, or is provenance yours alone? §5.4.

**O3 — Plaintext rules, or sealed ones?** A readable `automation_rules` table
lets the server (and an operator) see that you route CRED Club to your flat. The
alternative is a client-sealed blob, at the cost of the server never being able
to help with anything. My default is plaintext. §6.1.
